Business Problem

Before fire season begins, most homeowners lack the guidance, resources, and structured support needed to properly harden their homes. As a result, when wildfires strike, communities face the crisis unprepared. The immediate emotional response is fear and panic — people worry about their homes, their loved ones, and their own safety. In these critical moments, homeowners often feel powerless, unsure of what they could have done differently to protect what matters most.

Communication during wildfires is fragmented and unreliable, coming from inconsistent or delayed sources such as social media. Without a clear, trusted plan for preparation or real-time guidance, families experience devastating losses. When a home burns down, the memories and stability tied to it often feel lost as well. In the aftermath, many individuals struggle with grief while lacking a clear path forward. This cycle — little preparation, chaotic communication, and repeated loss — continues year after year, with no standardized system in place to help communities become truly wildfire-ready.
